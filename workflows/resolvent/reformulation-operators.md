# Reformulation operators

Select transformations that change the substance of the inquiry. These are construction materials, not a fixed three-part output taxonomy. Keep the current question available as the reference point for checking what changed.

## Separate the concern from its current carrier

Identify the institution, proxy, or activity currently delivering something valued. Explore how the concern could be pursued if that carrier stopped being necessary. Employment may carry income, contribution, belonging, and recognition, but those purposes need not change together. Do not decide which one the person truly values without evidence.

## Remove a necessity

Identify the condition that creates the original problem. Relax that condition explicitly and reconstruct what remains unresolved. Ask from inside the new condition. Do not quietly relax unrelated limits.

## Change the unit of inquiry

Move from a task to the obligations it sustains, from an individual to a relationship, from an output to a maintained capability, or from a publication to a claim's useful life. Choose the unit because it makes a consequential distinction expressible. Explain what the old unit loses and what the new unit can miss.

## Change who adapts or acts

Examine why the individual must adapt to an institution, why a permanent organization must coordinate an activity, or why a single person must sustain an inquiry. Reassign responsibility as an explicit proposal. Do not assume authority, consent, or benefit distribution follows from technical capability.

## Change the time structure

Convert an isolated event into a continuing process, preserve consequential paths beyond one project, or consider when an inquiry should pause, expire, or resume. Continuing computation does not imply unlimited attention, funding, physical access, or indefinite relevance.

## Distinguish chosen participation from necessary labor

Consider which activities the person may value doing, even if execution can be delegated. Treat mastery, experience, care, play, and agency as possible values, not universal diagnoses. A technically efficient substitution can change the concern itself.

## Allow an inherited objective to dissolve

Determine whether an objective mainly exists to cope with a constraint that the scenario removes. Reconstruct a meaningful question from the remaining concern. Keep any practical need that the reconstruction ceases to address visible.

## Introduce a newly possible concern

Imagine what a person already inhabiting the stipulated conditions could meaningfully want or need to investigate. The result should express a possibility unavailable in the original action space, not simply request more of an existing good. Make the enabling conditions and the resulting unresolved issue concrete.

## Examine the remaining limit

After relaxing one constraint, locate what becomes scarce, contested, or difficult. It might be trustworthy observation, attention, judgment, legitimacy, physical resources, consent, distribution, or the ability to choose. Avoid making scarcity disappear by definition.

## Inspect the framing itself

Ask whether the original question combines different phenomena or depends on an unstable category. Construct a formulation that can investigate the category's applicability or failure. Do not use abstraction to abandon the human's actual purpose.

## Test relations between directions

The three surviving directions can be complementary, nested, tensioned, or appropriate under different conditions. State the relationship only if it helps the reader understand a real difference. Never manufacture disagreement or merge them into a fourth proposed question.

## Grounding and source boundaries

The founding design treats questions as provisional expressions of a concern within an assumed world. It combines problem refusal, construction from operational properties, deliberate resistance to early convergence, scrutiny of hidden assumptions, and preservation of residue. Apply these as inquiry practices, not as claims that established methods are invalid or that unusual constructions must be true.

Keep the original human wording and user-provided evidence available for comparison. If storing lineage is requested, record the changed assumption, conditions, preserved or reconsidered concern, and residual tension. Questions have conditions of usefulness; they should not be assigned a probability of truth as if they were factual claims.
